原文

5 段

It was the best of times, it was the worst of times, it was the age of wisdom, it was the age of foolishness, it was the epoch of belief, it was the epoch of incredulity, it was the season of Light, it was the season of Darkness, it was the spring of hope, it was the winter of despair, we had everything before us, we had nothing before us, we were all going direct to Heaven, we were all going direct the other way--in short, the period was so far like the present period, that some of its noisiest authorities insisted on its being received, for good or for evil, in the superlative degree of comparison only.

There were a king with a large jaw and a queen with a plain face, on the throne of England; there were a king with a large jaw and a queen with a fair face, on the throne of France. In both countries it was clearer than crystal to the lords of the State preserves of loaves and fishes, that things in general were settled for ever.

It was the year of Our Lord one thousand seven hundred and seventy-five. Spiritual revelations were conceded to England at that favoured period, as at this. Mrs. Southcott had recently attained her five-and-twentieth blessed birthday, of whom a prophetic private in the Life Guards had heralded the sublime appearance by announcing that arrangements were made for the swallowing up of London and Westminster. Even the Cock-lane ghost had been laid only a round dozen of years, after rapping out its messages, as the spirits of this very year last past (supernaturally deficient in originality) rapped out theirs. Mere messages in the earthly order of events had lately come to the English Crown and People, from a congress of British subjects in America: which, strange to relate, have proved more important to the human race than any communications yet received through any of the chickens of the Cock-lane brood.

France, less favoured on the whole as to matters spiritual than her sister of the shield and trident, rolled with exceeding smoothness down hill, making paper money and spending it. Under the guidance of her Christian pastors, she entertained herself, besides, with such humane achievements as sentencing a youth to have his hands cut off, his tongue torn out with pincers, and his body burned alive, because he had not kneeled down in the rain to do honour to a dirty procession of monks which passed within his view, at a distance of some fifty or sixty yards. It is likely enough that, rooted in the woods of France and Norway, there were growing trees, when that sufferer was put to death, already marked by the Woodman, Fate, to come down and be sawn into boards, to make a certain movable framework with a sack and a knife in it, terrible in history. It is likely enough that in the rough outhouses of some tillers of the heavy lands adjacent to Paris, there were sheltered from the weather that very day, rude carts, bespattered with rustic mire, snuffed about by pigs, and roosted in by poultry, which the Farmer, Death, had already set apart to be his tumbrils of the Revolution. But that Woodman and that Farmer, though they work unceasingly, work silently, and no one heard them as they went about with muffled tread: the rather, forasmuch as to entertain any suspicion that they were awake, was to be atheistical and traitorous.

In England, there was scarcely an amount of order and protection to justify much national boasting.

现代译文

那是最好的时代，那是最坏的时代；那是智慧的时代，那是愚昧的时代；那是信仰的时代，那是怀疑的时代；那是光明的季节，那是黑暗的季节；那是希望的春天，那是绝望的冬天；我们面前什么都有，我们面前什么也没有；我们全都直奔天堂而去，我们全都直奔另一个方向而去——简而言之，那个时代与现在这个时代如此相似，以至于一些最喧嚣的权威人士坚持认为，它只能被当作最高级的比较来接受，无论好坏。

英国王座上坐着一位下巴宽大的国王，和一位面容普通的王后；法国王座上坐着一位下巴宽大的国王，和一位面容俊美的王后。在两国，那些掌管面包和鱼的国库贵族们，看得比水晶还要清楚，一切事情都已经永远定局了。

那是主后一千七百七十五年。在那个受宠的时期，正如在这一时期一样，英国也承认了精神启示。南科特夫人最近刚满二十二岁，近卫骑兵团的一名预言者曾宣布，已经为吞并伦敦和威斯敏斯特做好了安排。就连科克巷幽灵也不过在十二年前才销声匿迹，而今年的鬼魂（在超自然的原创性上显得贫乏）刚刚敲出了它们的消息。在尘世的秩序中，最近也向英国王室和人民传达了来自美洲一群英国臣民的会议消息；奇怪的是，这些消息对人类而言，比通过科克兰鬼魂所传来的任何消息都更为重要。

法国，总体而言在精神事务上不如她的盾牌与三叉戟的姐妹国幸运，却顺顺当当地滑向了下坡，制造纸币并挥霍纸币。在基督教牧师的引导下，她还以这样的人道成就自娱：判处一名青年割去双手，用钳子撕出舌头，再将身体烧死，因为他没有在雨中跪下，以表示对五十或六十码外经过的肮脏僧侣队伍的敬意。很可能，在那个受刑者被处决时，法国与挪威的森林中，已经生长着一些被名为“命运”的樵夫标记的树木，它们将被锯成木板，用来制造一个带有袋子和刀子的可移动框架，这在历史上是可怕的。很可能，在巴黎附近一些耕作重地的简陋棚屋里，当天正有粗糙的运货马车被风雨遮蔽，这些马车沾满乡土泥泞，被猪围着打转，被家禽栖息，而名为“死亡”的农夫早已将它们指定为革命的囚车。但那个樵夫和那个农夫，虽然日夜不停地工作，却始终沉默不语，没人听见他们踏着无声的步伐前行。正因如此，若有人怀疑他们已经醒来，便会被视为无神论者和叛徒。

在英国，几乎没有什么秩序和保护足以值得全国自豪。

创作背景

《双城记》发表于1859年，以法国大革命前后的伦敦和巴黎为舞台。狄更斯借历史动荡讨论不公、暴力、牺牲与重生。

英国小说家，善于以鲜明人物和城市叙事呈现社会生活。
