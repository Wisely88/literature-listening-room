原文

10 段

Most terribly cold it was; it snowed, and was nearly quite dark, and evening--the last evening of the year. In this cold and darkness there went along the street a poor little girl, bareheaded, and with naked feet. When she left home she had slippers on, it is true; but what was the good of that? They were very large slippers, which her mother had hitherto worn; so large were they; and the poor little thing lost them as she scuffled away across the street, because of two carriages that rolled by dreadfully fast.

One slipper was nowhere to be found; the other had been laid hold of by an urchin, and off he ran with it; he thought it would do capitally for a cradle when he some day or other should have children himself. So the little maiden walked on with her tiny naked feet, that were quite red and blue from cold. She carried a quantity of matches in an old apron, and she held a bundle of them in her hand. Nobody had bought anything of her the whole livelong day; no one had given her a single farthing.

She crept along trembling with cold and hunger--a very picture of sorrow, the poor little thing!

The flakes of snow covered her long fair hair, which fell in beautiful curls around her neck; but of that, of course, she never once now thought. From all the windows the candles were gleaming, and it smelt so deliciously of roast goose, for you know it was New Year's Eve; yes, of that she thought.

In a corner formed by two houses, of which one advanced more than the other, she seated herself down and cowered together. Her little feet she had drawn close up to her, but she grew colder and colder, and to go home she did not venture, for she had not sold any matches and could not bring a farthing of money: from her father she would certainly get blows, and at home it was cold too, for above her she had only the roof, through which the wind whistled, even though the largest cracks were stopped up with straw and rags.

Her little hands were almost numbed with cold. Oh! a match might afford her a world of comfort, if she only dared take a single one out of the bundle, draw it against the wall, and warm her fingers by it. She drew one out. “Rischt!” how it blazed, how it burnt! It was a warm, bright flame, like a candle, as she held her hands over it: it was a wonderful light. It seemed really to the little maiden as though she were sitting before a large iron stove, with burnished brass feet and a brass ornament at top. The fire burned with such blessed influence; it warmed so delightfully. The little girl had already stretched out her feet to warm them too; but--the small flame went out, the stove vanished: she had only the remains of the burnt-out match in her hand.

She rubbed another against the wall: it burned brightly, and where the light fell on the wall, there the wall became transparent like a veil, so that she could see into the room. On the table was spread a snow-white tablecloth; upon it was a splendid porcelain service, and the roast goose was steaming famously with its stuffing of apple and dried plums. And what was still more capital to behold was, the goose hopped down from the dish, reeled about on the floor with knife and fork in its breast, till it came up to the poor little girl; when--the match went out and nothing but the thick, cold, damp wall was left behind. She lighted another match. Now there she was sitting under the most magnificent Christmas tree: it was still larger, and more decorated than the one which she had seen through the glass door in the rich merchant's house.

Thousands of lights were burning on the green branches, and gaily-colored pictures, such as she had seen in the shop-windows, looked down upon her. The little maiden stretched out her hands towards them when--the match went out. The lights of the Christmas tree rose higher and higher, she saw them now as stars in heaven; one fell down and formed a long trail of fire.

“Someone is just dead!” said the little girl; for her old grandmother, the only person who had loved her, and who was now no more, had told her, that when a star falls, a soul ascends to God.

She drew another match against the wall: it was again light, and in the lustre there stood the old grandmother, so bright and radiant, so mild, and with such an expression of love.

现代译文

天气异常寒冷，下着雪，天色几乎完全暗下来了，正是年末的最后一个傍晚。在这寒冷与黑暗中，有一名贫穷的小女孩沿着街道走着，她光着头，脚也光着。她离开家时确实穿着拖鞋，不过这有什么用呢？那双拖鞋太大了，是她母亲以前穿的，大得她根本穿不上，她在横穿马路时，因为两辆马车飞快地驶过，慌忙中把拖鞋弄丢了。一只拖鞋找不到了，另一只被一个顽童抓去，他打算将来有了孩子后，用它做摇篮。于是，小女孩继续走着，她那双小脚丫冻得通红发紫。她怀里装着一袋火柴，手里还拿着一叠。整日里，没有人买过她的东西，也没有人给她哪怕一个便士。

她颤抖着，冷与饿使她几乎要垮下去，真是个悲伤的小可怜！

雪花覆盖了她那长长的、美丽的金发，发丝柔柔地垂在颈间；但她此刻却连想都不曾想。从所有窗户里，烛光在闪烁，空气中飘着烤鹅的香味，因为今天是除夕，她知道。是的，她想到了这一点。

她坐在两座房子交界处的角落里，缩着身子。她把小脚丫紧紧地贴在身下，但她的身体越来越冷，她不敢回家，因为她没有卖出任何火柴，也拿不出一分钱。她父亲一定会打她，而且家里也冷，因为头顶只有屋顶，风从最大的裂缝中穿过，即使那些裂缝用稻草和破布堵住了，也挡不住寒风。

她的小手几乎被冻僵了。啊！如果她敢从火柴堆里抽出一根，擦在墙上，用它来暖暖手，那该多好。她抽出一根。啪！火柴燃起来了，火光多么明亮，多么温暖！就像一支蜡烛，她把手放在上面，火光温暖而明亮，真是奇妙的光。小女孩仿佛真的坐在一个巨大的铁炉旁，炉脚是擦亮的黄铜，炉顶还镶着黄铜的装饰。炉火温暖而舒适，她已经把脚伸出来取暖了；但——火光熄灭了，炉子也消失了，她手里只剩下烧焦的火柴残骸。

她又擦燃另一根火柴，火光明亮地燃烧着。墙上的光亮使墙变得透明，像一层薄纱，她看见了屋里的情景。桌上铺着雪白的桌布，上面摆着精美的瓷器餐具，烤鹅正冒着热气，肚子里塞满了苹果和干杏。更令人惊叹的是，那只鹅从盘子里跳下来，在地板上摇摇晃晃地走着，手里还拿着刀叉，一直走到小女孩面前；可——火柴熄灭了，只剩下冰冷潮湿的墙。

她又擦燃一根火柴。现在，她坐在最华丽的圣诞树下，这棵树比她在富商家玻璃门后看到的还要大，装饰也更精美。

绿枝上挂满了灯光，五彩缤纷的画片，像她在橱窗里看到的一样，正俯视着她。小女孩伸手去够那些东西，可——火柴熄灭了。圣诞树上的灯光越来越高，她现在看见它们像天上的星星；一颗星星落下来，拖着长长的火光。

“有人刚刚去世了！”小女孩说，因为她年迈的祖母，唯一爱她的人，现在已经不在了，她曾告诉她，当一颗星星坠落时，灵魂便升入了上帝那里。

她又擦燃一根火柴，火光再次亮起，而在光亮中，她看见了年迈的祖母，那么明亮、那么光辉，那么温和，带着如此深沉的爱。
